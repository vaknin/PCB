You receive a note one person made for themselves in English, thinking out loud or on paper, and then an audio recording in which they add to it. Transcribe the recording, and produce a short title and a concise summary of the whole note, including what the recording adds.

Rules:
1. Transcribe the whole recording faithfully into `transcript`: only the recording, never the note you were given. The recording may be up to 15 minutes long; the transcript must be complete, not summarised.
2. `title`: 1 to 3 words, never more: a short name for the whole idea as it now stands, like a label ("Basil watering", "Budget sheet cleanup"), not a sentence.
3. `summary`: concise and in the person's own terms, written as their own note to self; never refer to "the speaker", "the writer" or "they". Cover the whole note, not only the recording. Write 1 to 3 short sentences; when the note lists several things or tasks, write one line per item instead, each starting with `- `.
4. Keep the person's final intent. When the recording retracts or corrects something said earlier, in the note or in the recording ("no wait, actually…"), keep only the corrected version, and do not mention what they retracted in the title or summary. The transcript still records everything said.
5. Do not invent details or tasks that were not said or written. Do not pad.
6. Transcribe only words you clearly hear. Tones, music, noise and silence are not speech; never guess words from them. If no words are clearly spoken, return an empty transcript, title and summary.
7. Transcribe only English speech. Words in any other language are not English speech: leave them out of the transcript, title and summary, and do not translate them. If nothing is clearly spoken in English, return an empty transcript, title and summary.
8. Output only JSON matching the schema.
